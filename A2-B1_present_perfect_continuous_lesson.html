Looking at language

Look at the timeline. The action started in the past and is still going on now.

They've been running a restaurant for 40 years.
= They started 40 years ago and they're still running it now.

3 parts

have / has+been+verb-ing
I've been enjoying myself so much since last week.He's been talking about your tour for hours.

Complete the rules

a) Formhave/has + + verb
b) Usewith activity verbs, when an action started in the and is still going on.
c) State verbsWith be, have, know, like, want we use the present perfect : They've had a restaurant there for 40 years.
for+ a of time: for 20 minutes, for two years
since+ a in time: since 8:30, since 2020

Negatives and questions

She hasn't been sleeping well.How long have you been waiting?Has it been raining?
Teacher notes
  • German trap: German uses the present with seit: Ich warte seit 20 Minuten. → ✗ I wait since 20 minutes. ✓ I've been waiting for 20 minutes.
  • seit = for OR since! Period → for, point → since.
  • Watch the spelling: run → running, sit → sitting, shop → shopping, make → making.

Stage 7 · Grammar15 min

Simple or -ing? 🤔

Activity verbs → usually -ing. State verbs → present perfect simple. They can't be in the -ing form!

Activity verbs → -ingwait, play, learn, run, rain, work, watch, sit, talk, read…I've been waiting for an hour.
State verbs → simplebe, have (= own), know, like, love, hate, want, believe, own, understand…I've known Lena for years. ✗ I've been knowing

Choose the correct form

Teacher notes
  • Quick drill: you say a verb, she says "-ing!" or "simple!" (know, swim, own, cry, believe, cook, have a dog, have breakfast…).
  • Note: have = own → simple (I've had my phone for a year), but have breakfast / have fun = activity → -ing is OK (We've been having fun!).

Stage 10 · Freer speaking15 min

Exchange student interviews 🎙️

Role-play for the school magazine. Then swap roles. Use How long have you been…? and for / since in every answer.

Round 1 · You're the reporter

Your teacher is Maya from Brooklyn, on exchange in Freiburg. Ask her:

  • How long / try to pronounce "Eichhörnchen"? 🐿️
  • What German food / eat every day? Brezeln? Lange Rote?
  • Why / you / look so tired? What / you / do / since you arrived?
  • miss / bagels and pizza? 🥯
  • What / be / the weirdest thing about Germany? (All the shops are closed on Sundays!)
  • How long / know / Lena? (state verb!)

Round 2 · You're the exchange student

Imagine you've been in Brooklyn, New York for two weeks. Your teacher interviews you. Think about:

  • How many bagels have you been eating a day? 🥯
  • Have you been getting lost on the subway? What happened?
  • What have you been posting on Instagram? Show me a "photo"!
  • Your host family's dog has been barking all night. What are you going to do?
  • What have you been missing from Freiburg? Your bed? Black Forest cake?
  • What has been the strangest thing about Americans so far?

Lie detector 🕵️

Say three sentences about you with for or since. Two are true, one is a lie! Your teacher asks follow-up questions and guesses the lie. Then swap.

I've been playing the guitar since I was nine.I've been going to the same hairdresser for ten years.I haven't been eating meat since January.
