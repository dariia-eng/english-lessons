3The -s rules

15 min

Step 1: Who gets the -s? Game

he / she / it → -s ✓I / you / we / they → no -s

Look at each person. Do we add -s to the verb? Say a sentence: "My mum wakes up at 7."

Help: my mum = she, Zoe = she, my cat = it, my friends = they, Sam and Leo = they.

Step 2: How do we add it? Three rules ✏️

Rule 1 Most verbs: +

wake → wakes · get → gets · play → plays · love → loves

Rule 2 Verbs ending in -sh, -ch, -o: +

brush → brushes · watch → watches · go → goes · do → does

Rule 3 have is crazy! →

I have breakfast. → She ??? breakfast. (Look at Zoe's text!)
⭐ Bonus, just look: some verbs end in consonant + y. Then y → ies: study → studies, tidy → tidies. We practise this next time!

Elicit each rule from the verb hunt: "Look: watches, goes, brushes. Why -es?" Pronunciation: watches, brushes have an extra syllable /ɪz/.

I → Zoe

Read the sentence about you. Now write the verb for Zoe or her family.

  1. I wake up at 7.Zoe up at 7.
  2. I have breakfast at 7.30.She breakfast at 7.30.
  3. I listen to music.Her mum to music in the car.
  4. I go to school by bus.Zoe to school by bike.
  5. I start school at 9.She school at 8.30.
  6. I watch films on Friday.Leo cartoons every day.
  7. I play computer games.Her dad the guitar.
  8. I do my homework after dinner.Zoe her homework after school.
  9. I brush my teeth after breakfast.She her teeth before bed.
  10. I chat online with my friends.Zoe online with her fans.

Then speaking: she reads both columns aloud, "I wake up at 7, and Zoe wakes up at 7 too."

Me vs my best friend Speaking + writing

Tell the teacher about your day and your best friend's day. Then write 3 sentences on each side. The checker looks at your -s!

The checker only looks at the first verb after I/she/he, so read the sentences together too.
