Grammar: Mistakes in the Past

⏱ 15 minutes — read the grammar box first!

📖 Grammar Reference

1. wish / if only + past perfect → regret about the past
wish / if only + had/hadn't + past participle
→ I wish I hadn't bought that useless gadget.
→ If only I had read the reviews first.
⚠️ NOT: I wish I didn't buy / I wish I haven't bought
2. should have / ought to have + past participle → criticism or regret
should/ought to + have + past participle
→ You should have saved money before moving out.
→ I shouldn't have spent so much on that jacket.
3. could have + past participle → missed opportunity in the past
could + have + past participle
→ I could have got a cheaper flight if I'd booked earlier.
4. was / were supposed to + verb → planned but didn't happen
was/were supposed to + infinitive
→ We were supposed to split the bill, but he disappeared.
→ You weren't supposed to spend all of it!
🎯 Choose the correct option to complete each sentence.

1. I _______ that gaming chair. It broke after two months.

2. If only I _______ the reviews before ordering from that sketchy website.

3. She _______ more money before moving to London. It's so expensive.

4. He _______ those concert tickets weeks ago — now they're double the price.

5. I wish I _______ all my savings on that useless online course.

Pronunciation: Connected Speech

⏱ 8 minutes
🎧 Listen to the conversation. How do the speakers pronounce should have and could have? Then practise with a partner.

📖 Connected Speech: reducing "have"

In natural spoken English, should have and could have are almost never said as two full words. The word have reduces to /əv/ — it blends with the modal before it:

should have → /ˈʃʊdəv/ sounds like "shoulda" or "should've"
could have → /ˈkʊdəv/ sounds like "coulda" or "could've"
⚠️ This is spoken English only — never write shoulda or coulda in formal writing!

Conversation — read and listen, then roleplay in pairs. The highlighted words show where sounds reduce:

A: How did your exam go?

B: It could've been worse. (/ˈkʊdəv/)

A: It could've been worse?

B: Yeah, I should've done better. (/ˈʃʊdəv/)

A: You should've done better?

B: Look, this is supposed to be a holiday!

A: And you were supposed to do well in the exam!

B: Yeah, well, it could've been worse.

💡 Practise tip: Say "could've been worse" five times fast — the have should almost disappear! Now try "should've done better" the same way.
💬 Work in pairs and roleplay the conversation — copy the pronunciation. Then switch roles.
