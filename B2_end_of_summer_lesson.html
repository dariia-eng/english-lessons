Stage 3 · 12 min

📖 Three Summers — Which One Is You?

Read quickly, then we talk. The reading is a springboard — not an exercise.

This summer was a genuine watershed moment for me. For two years I had been working without a break, and I finally gave myself permission to stop and take stock of my life. Three weeks in the south of France — long mornings, no emails, no plans. It was bittersweet in a way: I felt relieved to be resting, yet slightly guilty about how long I had been ignoring the things that matter most. I reminisced a great deal about simpler times as a student, when life felt lighter and slower. By the end of the holiday I had made a quiet but firm decision: I am going to work less and live more deliberately.

If I am honest, this summer was a disappointment — and that is mostly my own fault. I should have booked time off in June instead of waiting until August, when everywhere was crowded and expensive. I should have called my friends more instead of assuming they were busy. Those evenings we did manage to meet felt so fleeting; before I had a chance to truly enjoy them, they were over. I tend to dwell on what I didn't do, and I know that isn't useful. I wish I had been more spontaneous, more present. But I suppose recognising that honestly is itself a form of progress.

I hadn't expected this summer to mean very much. No travel, no big events, nothing dramatic. But in the middle of July, sitting alone in my garden at dusk, I had a quiet epiphany. I realised I hadn't felt lonely once all summer. That surprised me enormously. I had always believed I needed people around me to feel happy — yet here I was, entirely content in my own company. I feel wistful that it's ending, but mostly I feel curious. Autumn suddenly seems full of possibility, and I wish I had discovered this sooner. Still — better late than never.

Confession card
Press "Next card" to begin

Language reminder — wish & should have

I wish I had + V3 — personal regret about the past
"I wish I had called more often."

I should have + V3 — recognising the right action was missed
"I should have booked earlier."

Both are fine — sometimes either works. Use whichever feels natural.

Stage 5 · 12 min

💬 Big Questions

Open discussion — no right answers. Aim to develop each response for 1–2 minutes.

1
Is summer overrated? We put so much pressure on it to be perfect — is that fair?
Follow-up: Where does the pressure come from — social media, childhood expectations, something else?
2
Why do we always look back on time we didn't use "well"? What does "well" even mean?
Follow-up: Is there such a thing as a wasted summer, or is any experience worthwhile?
3
Some people say autumn is the real new year — fresh start, new routines. Do you feel that way?
Follow-up: What do you actually want to be different this autumn compared to last year?
4
Would you want to remember every summer in perfect detail — or is it good that some things fade?
Follow-up: What's the value of a fleeting memory — one you can barely hold onto?
5
"The best summers are the ones you barely planned." True or false?
Follow-up: Give an example from your own life that supports or challenges this.

Useful language for these discussions

I tend to dwell on… It was bittersweet because… I wish I had… I should have… Those moments felt so fleeting… In hindsight… It was a watershed moment… Taking stock of it now… I had an epiphany that… Looking back, I feel wistful about…
