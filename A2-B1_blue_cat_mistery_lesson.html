Stage 3 · Reading15 min

The night the painting disappeared

Read the case file. Then answer the questions.

Friday, 9:15 p.m. · Freiburg

It was a cold, rainy Friday night in Freiburg. The Schwarzwald Art Museum, just around the corner from the Münster, was closing, but four people were still inside.

In the main hall, Petra, the cleaner, was mopping the floor. Max, the night guard, was sitting in his little room next to the entrance. Upstairs, Professor Bauer was working in his office, and Leo, a young artist, was taking photos of the paintings for his Instagram.

Outside, it was raining heavily and the Bächle in the street were full of water. At 9:15 there was a huge flash of lightning over the Schlossberg, and suddenly all the lights went out. While everyone was looking for a torch, somebody screamed. Five minutes later, when the lights came back on, Petra was pointing at the wall. Somebody had taken the museum's most famous painting, The Blue Cat!

Max called the police. Detective Lena Fischer arrived at ten o'clock. Nobody had left the building, so she knew one thing: the thief was still inside. "Nobody goes home," she said. "I want to know exactly what everyone was doing at 9:15."

Did you understand?

Stage 4 · Grammar20 min

Three past tenses for telling a story

"Petra was mopping when the lights went out. When the detective arrived, the thief had already hidden the painting."

Past continuouswas / were + -ing
the background, a long action in progress
Past simplewent, arrived, screamed
the main events, one after another
Past perfecthad + past participle
"the past before the past": it happened earlier

Check the meaning

Rule card · complete it

Past continuousI / he / she / it + + verb-ing · you / we / they + + verb-ing
Question were you doing at 9:15?
when / whilewhen + past (short action) · while + past (long action)
Past perfect + past participle (taken, left, hidden) · for ALL persons: I had, she had, they had
Past perfect negativeLeo said he (not / be) downstairs. But he had!

Spot the mistake 🔍

Teacher notes
  • German trap #1: German uses Perfekt for finished past (Ich habe ihn gestern gesehen). In English, a finished time word (yesterday, last week, at 9:15) means past simple: I saw him yesterday.
  • German trap #2: German has no continuous form, so she may say "At 9:15 I watched TV" for an action in progress. Push for was watching.
  • German als = when (past single event), während = while. This helps a lot with when/while.
  • Past perfect: only use it when the order of events matters. Don't let her overuse it.

Freiburg Daily News · Saturday

Blue Cat found in bike trailer!

Freiburg police (find) the famous painting The Blue Cat! Detective Lena Fischer (arrest) the thief late last night. "When we (arrive) at the museum, the thief (already / hide) the painting in his bike trailer," she said. "And while we (talk) to the suspects, the rain (stop)." The museum usually (open) at 10 a.m. Tomorrow, The Blue Cat (be) back on the wall, and next Saturday the museum (have) a big party. The plans are ready! Max, the night guard, says: "I (never / be) so tired in my life. Right now I (drink) my fifth coffee!"
Teacher notes
  • A: after checking, she reads the whole text aloud. Listen for weak was /wəz/ and had → 'd.
  • D: for each gap ask "Which word told you?" (found → news/now result; last night → past simple; tomorrow → will; plans are ready → going to; never → present perfect).
  • D1 is a nice German contrast: news + no finished time → present perfect is correct here!
