Beyond the Horizon: The New Age of Space Tourism

For most of human history, space travel was an experience that only highly trained government astronauts could have. That is now changing. A new generation of private companies, whose ambition is to make space accessible to paying passengers, is transforming the industry at an extraordinary pace.

In 2021, a milestone was reached. A fully civilian crew completed a three-day orbit of Earth. The four passengers, who had no formal astronaut training, returned safely — proving that space was no longer exclusively reserved for professionals. Their capsule, which had been built by a private aerospace company, splashed down in the Atlantic Ocean.

One of the greatest challenges that engineers currently face is the cost of a launch. A seat a paying customer books on an orbital flight can still cost millions of dollars. However, companies whose business models depend on reusable rockets are working hard to drive prices down. A rocket that can land itself after delivering cargo and be flown again within weeks represents a fundamental shift in how the industry operates.

Scientists refer to the "overview effect" as the powerful emotional response that astronauts feel when they first see Earth floating in the darkness of space. This shift in perspective, which researchers have documented in interviews with dozens of astronauts, is said to produce a lasting sense of responsibility towards our planet.

The next frontier is the orbital hotel — a concept that several companies are racing to turn into reality. Guests would live in pressurised modules that offer unobstructed views of Earth below. Critics, whose objections focus on the environmental footprint of rocket launches, argue that the carbon cost is difficult to justify. The debate, which shows no sign of slowing down, reflects a much deeper question about the future of exploration.

Whatever your view, the industry that many people once considered pure science fiction is becoming part of everyday reality — just a rather expensive reality.

Relative Clauses

Relative clauses give additional information about people, things and places. The clause comes directly after the noun and begins with a relative pronoun: who, which, that, where, when or whose. Relative clauses never use what.

Defining Relative Clauses

The information is essential — it identifies which person or thing is meant. Without the clause, the sentence loses its core meaning.

A rocket that can land itself represents a revolution in spaceflight.
The passengers who had no astronaut training proved anyone could do it. (→ that can replace who)

When the relative pronoun is the object of the clause, it can be left out:

A seat (that) a customer books costs millions of dollars.

Non-defining Relative Clauses

The information is extra — interesting but not essential to identify the noun.

The capsule, which had been built privately, splashed down safely.
The crew, who had no formal training, returned safely.

⚠️ Always separated by commas.
⚠️ You cannot use that in a non-defining clause.
⚠️ You cannot omit the relative pronoun in a non-defining clause.

10 Sentence transformation

Combine the two sentences into one using a relative clause. Think carefully about defining vs non-defining, and punctuate accordingly.

1
Neil Armstrong was a pioneering astronaut. He walked on the Moon in July 1969.
Model: Neil Armstrong was a pioneering astronaut who walked on the Moon in July 1969.
2
The James Webb Space Telescope was launched in December 2021. It has taken the deepest images of the universe ever captured.
Model: The James Webb Space Telescope, which was launched in December 2021, has taken the deepest images of the universe ever captured.
3
I read about a scientist. Her research focuses on making Mars atmospherically habitable for humans.
Model: I read about a scientist whose research focuses on making Mars atmospherically habitable for humans.
4
Kazakhstan has a famous spaceport. Rockets have been launched from there since the 1950s.
Model: Kazakhstan has a famous spaceport where rockets have been launched since the 1950s.
5
2021 was a historic year for private spaceflight. The first fully civilian crew orbited Earth in that year.
Model: 2021 was a historic year for private spaceflight, when the first fully civilian crew orbited Earth.
