P — Pronunciation
Teacher: Model each contraction clearly. Drill the weak form of would → /wəd/. Students repeat each sentence with natural stress.

In natural speech, would contracts with the subject pronoun.

Full form Contracted Phonetic Example
I wouldI'd/aɪd/I'd go to work by train.
You wouldYou'd/juːd/You'd feel better without it.
He / She wouldHe'd / She'd/hiːd/ /ʃiːd/She'd read more books.
We / They wouldWe'd / They'd/wiːd/ /ðeɪd/We'd all be happier.
would notwouldn't/ˈwʊdnt/I wouldn't miss it.

Sentence stress — the important words carry the stress:

If I lived near the station, I'd go to work by train.
If we gave up social media, we'd sleep much better.
What would you do if you lost your phone?
💡 Weak form: would in natural speech is rarely stressed. It sounds like /wəd/ — very short and quiet. The contraction 'd sounds the same. Practise saying the sentences above at natural speed.

🔍 Spot the Mistake

Each sentence has one grammar mistake. Find it, then click to check.

1. "If I will have more time, I would cook more at home."
✅ "If I had more time, I would cook more at home." — use past simple in the if-clause.
2. "She would bought a robot vacuum if it wasn't so expensive."
✅ "She would buy a robot vacuum if it wasn't so expensive." — after would, use the base form.
3. "What would happen if everyone stops using smartphones?"
✅ "What would happen if everyone stopped using smartphones?" — the if-clause needs past simple (stopped), not present simple.
4. "If I was you, I would delete TikTok immediately."
✅ "If I were you, I would delete TikTok immediately." — "If I were you" is the standard form for giving advice.

Stage 6 · Feedback & Error Correction
Spot the mistake
Teacher: Add any errors you noted during Stage 5 to these prepared sentences. Students identify the mistake, then click to check. Discuss why each is wrong.

Each sentence contains a common second conditional mistake. Can you find and correct it?

1. "If I will have a car, I would drive to work."
✅ "If I had a car, I would drive to work."
Use past simple (had) in the if-clause — not 'will have'.
2. "I would went to the gym more if I wasn't so tired."
✅ "I would go to the gym more if I wasn't so tired."
After 'would', use the base form (go) — not past simple (went).
3. "If there was no internet, life would be more better."
✅ "If there was no internet, life would be much better / a lot better."
'Better' is already comparative — we can't add 'more'. Use 'much' or 'a lot' to intensify.
4. "What would you do if you will lose the internet for a week?"
✅ "What would you do if you lost the internet for a week?"
In the if-clause of a second conditional, use past simple (lost) — not 'will lose'.
5. "If I was you, I'd delete social media."
✅ "If I were you, I'd delete social media."
'If I were you' is a fixed phrase for giving advice — use 'were', not 'was'.
6. "She would be happier if she has more free time."
✅ "She would be happier if she had more free time."
Second conditional if-clause needs past simple (had) — not present simple (has).
