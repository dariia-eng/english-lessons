Language Focus: Verb Patterns
10 min
Remember: some verbs have more than one pattern. The pattern you use changes the meaning of the sentence.

Verb Patterns after Reporting Verbs

Pattern 1 — verb + infinitive with to
e.g. threaten, refuse, agree
She threatened to call the police. / He refused to leave.
Pattern 2 — verb + person + infinitive with to
e.g. remind, convince, advise
He reminded me not to say anything. / She advised him to leave.
Pattern 3 — verb + -ing form
e.g. deny, admit
He denied lying on his CV. / She admitted taking it.
Pattern 4 — verb + preposition + -ing
e.g. apologise for, insist on
I apologised for spoiling the evening. / He insisted on paying.
Pattern 5 — verb + person + preposition + -ing
e.g. blame for, accuse of
She blamed me for ordering the wrong food. / He accused her of lying.

Speaking: The Alex & Bo Situation
10 min

The Situation

Alex invited Bo to dinner. Alex spent a long time preparing and making things nice. Bo forgot about the arrangement and didn't turn up. The next day Bo sent a short text saying Sorry lol! Alex generally feels that Bo is not being a good friend at the moment. Bo never asks questions or shares personal information. Alex has decided to call Bo.

Step 1 — Read the opening lines of the conversation.

Alex: Why didn't you come for dinner last night?
Bo: Sorry, Alex. I was watching the game and I completely forgot.

Step 2 — Write the rest of the phone conversation. Think about what Alex might say and how Bo might respond. Use some of these verbs:

accuse · admit · advise · agree · apologise · blame · deny · insist · remind · threaten

Step 3 — Report the conversation. Tell your teacher what was said in the conversation. Use reporting verbs.

Example: Alex accused Bo of not caring about their friendship. Bo apologised for forgetting and admitted that he had been distracted. Alex reminded him that it wasn't the first time…
