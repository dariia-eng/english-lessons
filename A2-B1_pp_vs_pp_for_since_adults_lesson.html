How long have you lived abroad?

"I've lived abroad for… / I've lived here since…"
Question 2
How long have you snowboarded?
Where was your very first time on a board? What was it like? Has your style changed a lot since you started?
"I've snowboarded for… / I first tried it since…"
Question 3
How long have you played table tennis?
How did you get into it? Do you play more seriously now than when you started, or is it still just for fun?
📐 Grammar · for & since

The Key Difference

Both for and since are used with Present Perfect to talk about situations that started in the past and continue now. The difference is simple:

FORa period of time

Use for + a length of time — how long something has lasted.
for 2 years for a long time for ages for six months for my whole life for a week
I have worked here for three years.
She has known him for ages.
They haven't spoken for a week.

SINCEa starting point

Use since + the moment something started — a date, year, or event.
since 2020 since Monday since I was a child since we met since January since the beginning
I have worked here since 2021.
She has known him since university.
They haven't spoken since the argument.
The question is always the same: Are you saying how long (a period) → use for. Are you saying from when (a moment) → use since.

💬 "How long have you lived here?" → "For five years." · "Since 2019."

Always Moving

Lena is 31 and has lived in Austria (1) 2019 — she originally came for a ski season and simply never booked a flight home. She has snowboarded (2) almost ten years, but she says she has improved more (3) she moved to the mountains than in all the years before.

She has also played table tennis (4) university — she discovered it during a rainy week at a hostel in Vietnam and has been obsessed ever since. She hasn't been back to Vietnam (5) three years, but she says it is next on the list.

Lena has spoken German well enough to feel local (6) about two years now — and locals no longer ask where she is from, which she considers her greatest achievement.
