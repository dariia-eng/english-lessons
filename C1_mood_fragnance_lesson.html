Scent, mood & memory

A speaking lesson about mood fragrance, scent layering and nostalgic gourmands. We'll describe smells precisely, discuss what's trending, and practise talking about benefits without over-promising.

Stage 3 · Trend snapshots · 15 min

Hype or helpful?

Read each snapshot aloud, answer the question, then give your verdict. Use the phrases to give your opinion.

Teacher note
  • She reads one card aloud (pronunciation check), answers the question, then picks a verdict and justifies it.
  • Play devil's advocate to extend each answer. Aim for 2 minutes per card.
  • Note the cautious phrases she uses naturally; they lead into Stage 4.
I'd say it's mostly…It seems to me that…It might just be a phase.I suspect it's here to stay.In my experience, clients tend to…I'm not entirely convinced…
i.

Mood fragrance

Big brands now sell scents named Calm or Focus. Critics say the effect may come more from ritual and expectation than from chemistry.

Aromatherapists have known this for generations. Is it new, or just new marketing?

ii.

Scent layering

Instead of one signature perfume, people build a "scent wardrobe": a body oil, a mist and a perfume worn together.

Would you recommend layering to clients? Which combinations work?

iii.

Gourmand nostalgia

Pistachio, toasted sugar and warm milk are everywhere. Sweet, edible scents have become hugely popular.

Why do you think people want to smell like dessert right now?

iv.

Skin scents

Soft, musky fragrances that sit close to the body and smell "like you, but better".

Is a scent other people can barely smell still worth paying for?

v.

Upcycled ingredients

Some makers extract oils from what the food industry throws away, like citrus peel or coffee grounds.

Would "made from waste" make you more or less likely to buy?

vi.

AI-designed blends

A few brands use algorithms to suggest new formulas. Most perfumers say a trained nose can't be replaced.

Could AI ever do your job? Which part could it never do?

Stage 4 · Language focus · 8 min

Promising honestly: hedging

Makers should promise only what a scent can really deliver. In English we do this with hedging: language that makes a claim softer and more careful. It's essential for labels, websites and consultations.

Teacher note
  • Start with the cautious phrases she used in Stage 3, then complete the rule card together.
  • She probably knows the meaning, so focus on form and the typical errors below.

Rule card: complete the patterns

may / might / + infinitive This blend may ease tension.
is said / thought / believed + infinitive Neroli is thought to be calming.
It is believed + clause It is believed that scent and memory are closely linked.
There is some that + clause There is some evidence that peppermint helps alertness.
to + infinitive Woody notes tend to last longer on the skin.
It that + clause It seems that gourmand scents are here to stay.

Spot the mistake

1. Lavender is said help with sleep.Lavender is said to help with sleep.
2. This oil may to reduce stress.This oil may reduce stress. (no "to" after modals)
3. It is believed what peppermint helps concentration.It is believed that peppermint helps concentration.
4. Neroli is thought being calming.Neroli is thought to be calming.
