Phrases about ideas
Exercise 8 — Phrases about ideas
Study the nine phrases. Click CCQ on each to check your understanding.
it occurs to me
“It suddenly occurs to me that we haven’t tried role storming yet.”
Is this a sudden thought or a planned one? → Sudden — it just comes to you
Does the person choose to think of it? → No — it arrives on its own
springs to mind
“When I think of creative companies, Google springs to mind immediately.”
Is it fast or slow? → Fast and automatic
Is it triggered by something? → Yes — usually a topic or context
it dawned on me
“It suddenly dawned on me that we were solving the wrong problem.”
Is this a realisation or a plan? → A realisation — new understanding
Did they know this before? → No — it’s new to them
bounce ideas around
“Let’s bounce a few ideas around and see what comes up.”
Alone or with others? → With others — it’s collaborative
Fixed goal or exploratory? → Exploratory — you don’t know where it leads
a bright idea
“It was her bright idea to try reverse brainstorming.”
Good or bad idea? → Good / clever — but can be sarcastic
Sarcasm example: → “Oh, that was a bright idea…” (meaning it was NOT)
just a passing thought
“It’s just a passing thought, but what if we asked the customers directly?”
Is the speaker confident? → No — tentative, not pushy
Why use this phrase? → To soften a suggestion or test the reaction
out of ideas
“We’ve been brainstorming for an hour and I’m completely out of ideas.”
Does the person have ideas? → No — they’ve run out
Permanent or temporary? → Usually temporary
give someone an idea
“Watching children play gave me an idea for the whole campaign.”
Source of the idea? → Something external triggers it
Planned? → No — it happens naturally
a brainwave
“I had a brainwave in the shower — what if we reversed the whole process?”
Ordinary or special? → A sudden, particularly good idea
Planned? → No — comes unexpectedly

👑Game A — Hot Seat5–10 min
Student faces away or closes eyes. Teacher reveals a phrase and gives clues using question forms only. Then swap roles.
1Student faces away or covers the screen. Teacher taps to reveal a phrase.
2Teacher gives clues as questions only: “Is it something that happens suddenly?” “Don’t you think it sounds like a sunrise?”
3Student guesses the phrase. Then swap — teacher faces away, student gives clues.
🗣️Game B — Two Truths & a Brainwave8–12 min
Use the vocabulary to tell two true statements and one invented one about yourself. Your teacher listens and guesses the lie.
1Write 3 sentences using different vocabulary phrases from Exercise 8. Two are true; one is invented.
2Read them aloud. Your teacher asks follow-up questions using the question forms from today’s lesson to probe each statement.
3Teacher guesses which is invented. Then swap — your teacher tells three, you guess.
Example: “It once dawned on me during a flight that I was applying to the wrong job. / I had a brainwave in the shower that turned into an actual project. / It often occurs to me that I’m the only one who bounces ideas around at work.”

Exercise 12 — Advice column
Your teacher reads a problem out loud. You give practical advice — recommend a brainstorming technique and explain how to run it step by step. Use vocabulary from Stage 4 where you can.
📩 “Our weekly team meetings always feel like a waste of time. We talk for an hour and leave with no clear ideas. Help!”
📩 “I’m the only extrovert on my team. Every time I suggest something, nobody responds. What should I do?”
📩 “We’ve been trying to fix the same customer complaint for six months. We’ve run out of ideas. Any suggestions?”
📩 “I need to write a marketing campaign for a product I don’t really believe in. I can’t think creatively about it at all.”
Structure your advice: What I’d recommend in your situation is…  ·  The first step would be to…  ·  One thing to watch out for is…
Exercise 13 — 2-minute pitch
You are pitching a brainstorming initiative to your manager (your teacher). Choose one technique and convince them to adopt it. Your teacher will challenge you with tough questions.
Prepare (2 min)Choose your technique. Note: what problem does it solve? What are its advantages? What might go wrong?
Pitch (2 min)Deliver your pitch. Signpost your ideas clearly and use vocabulary from Stage 4.
Q&A (2 min)Your teacher (the manager) asks tough questions. Hold your ground — but concede where they have a point.
SwapYour teacher pitches a different technique. You play the sceptical manager.
Signposting
The reason I’m recommending this is…
The key advantage is…
I admit there are challenges, but…
Manager challenges
Isn’t this just a waste of time?
What evidence do you have that it works?
Why not just use email?

Exercise 16 — Mini-presentation
Prepare a 3-minute spoken summary of what you learned in these two lessons. Cover: which technique you find most interesting, a situation you’d use it in, and one thing that surprised you. Your teacher listens and then asks two follow-up questions.
Structure suggestion:
