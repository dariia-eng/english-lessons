Vocabulary: 12 Personality Adjectives
⏱ 10 minutes — read, repeat, remember
Read each card carefully. For each word — think of one person in your life who fits it. Tell your teacher!
confident
adjective
Sure of yourself; comfortable speaking and acting in difficult situations.
"She walks into every room like she owns it — so confident."
↔shy
shy
adjective
Nervous or uncomfortable around people you don't know well.
"He goes quiet at parties — he's very shy around strangers."
↔confident
generous
adjective
Happy to give your time, money, or help to other people.
"She always pays for everyone — so generous."
↔selfish
stubborn
adjective
Not willing to change your opinion or do what others want, even when they are right.
"He never admits he's wrong — so stubborn!"
↔flexible
ambitious
adjective
Wanting to be successful and achieve important things in life.
"She started her own business at 22 — very ambitious."
honest
adjective
Always telling the truth; not hiding or pretending.
"You can ask her anything — she's always honest with you."
↔dishonest
sociable
adjective
Enjoying spending time with other people; friendly and easy to talk to.
"He knows everyone at the party — so sociable!"
↔shy / antisocial
reliable
adjective
Someone you can always trust and count on — they do what they say.
"She's never late and always keeps her promises — very reliable."
↔unreliable
creative
adjective
Good at thinking of new ideas and making interesting or original things.
"Every post she makes is beautiful — so creative."
bossy
adjective
Always telling other people what to do, in an annoying way.
"In every group project she takes over — she's a bit bossy."
💡can be negative!
sensitive
adjective
Easily affected by what people say; feels emotions strongly.
"She cried when I said I didn't like the film — quite sensitive."
laid-back
adjective (informal)
Very relaxed; not worried or stressed about things.
"Nothing bothers him — he's the most laid-back person I know."
↔stressed / uptight

Exercise A — Which word fits?
⏱ 8 minutes — choose the best personality adjective
Read each situation. Choose the personality adjective that best describes this person.
1.
Emma always says exactly what she thinks. If she doesn't like your outfit, she'll tell you — kindly but honestly. Her friends appreciate this about her.
2.
Lucas is always the last one to arrive and often forgets to do the things he promised. His friends like him, but they've stopped counting on him for important things.
3.
Sofia has big plans. She's studying, working part-time, and already building her own brand on Instagram. At 24, she knows exactly where she wants to be in ten years.
4.
Marco hates conflict. He worries a lot about what other people think of him. When a friend makes a small joke about him, he gets upset for the rest of the day.
5.
Every group project, Carla takes charge. She decides what everyone does and how. Her ideas are usually good — but her group mates sometimes feel like they can't contribute.
6.
Tomás is the kind of person who never seems stressed. Missed the bus? "No problem, I'll take the next one." Cancelled plans last minute? "All good." Nothing seems to bother him.
7.
Anna never changes her mind. In the last argument with her partner, she spent three hours insisting she was right — even after he showed her the evidence.
8.
Lucia's content is always surprising — unusual angles, original ideas, beautiful colours. Every post looks like something you've never seen before.

Exercise C — Positive or Negative?
⏱ 6 minutes — context is everything!
Some personality words can be positive or negative depending on the situation. Choose the correct interpretation, then explain your answer in English!
1. "My boss is very stubborn."
In this context, does stubborn sound positive or negative?
2. "My 8-year-old niece is so bossy."
Is the speaker being positive or negative here?
3. "She's very sensitive about her work."
What does this most likely mean in a work context?
4. Which sentence uses laid-back in a negative way?
5. "She's incredibly ambitious."
This can be positive or negative depending on context. Which context makes it sound negative?
