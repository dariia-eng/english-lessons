📖Grammar: Comparatives & Superlatives
⏱ 10 minutes — read, understand, remember

We use comparatives to compare two people or things. We use superlatives to say who is the most/least in a group.

📐 Rule 1 — Short adjectives (1 syllable): add -er

AdjectiveComparativeSuperlative
shyshierthe shiest
youngyoungerthe youngest
talltallerthe tallest
oldolderthe oldest

📐 Rule 2 — Adjectives ending in -y: change to -ier / -iest

AdjectiveComparativeSuperlative
bossybossierthe bossiest
happyhappierthe happiest
funnyfunnierthe funniest

📐 Rule 3 — Long adjectives (2+ syllables): more / the most

AdjectiveComparativeSuperlative
confidentmore confidentthe most confident
ambitiousmore ambitiousthe most ambitious
reliablemore reliablethe most reliable
sociablemore sociablethe most sociable
creativemore creativethe most creative
sensitivemore sensitivethe most sensitive
generousmore generousthe most generous
stubbornmore stubbornthe most stubborn
laid-backmore laid-backthe most laid-back
honestmore honestthe most honest

⚡ Irregular — must memorise!

AdjectiveComparativeSuperlative
goodbetterthe best
badworsethe worst

💬 Sentence Patterns

COMPARATIVE
A is [comparative] than B  →  "My sister is more ambitious than me."
A is [comparative] than B  →  "Jake is shier than Mia."
SUPERLATIVE
A is the [superlative] in [group]  →  "She's the most creative person in our class."
A is the [superlative] of [group]  →  "He's the most laid-back of all my friends."
💡 Tip: Use than after a comparative ("more confident than you"), but use in / of after a superlative ("the most confident in the class").

📰Reading: Class Superlatives
⏱ 8 minutes — read and discuss

Every year, students at Westbrook High vote for the Class Superlatives — the "most" of everything. Read the profiles and answer the questions below — out loud!

🏆
Zara Ahmed — Most Ambitious
Year 10, age 15

Zara is the most ambitious student in Year 10 — and everyone knows it. She's already run two school fundraisers, started a climate club, and applied for a summer programme at a London university. Is she stressed? "Not really," she says with a smile. "I just love doing things." Her teachers say she's also one of the most reliable students they've ever had — she has never once missed a deadline.

🎨
Leo Park — Most Creative
Year 10, age 15

Leo is quietly the most creative person in the school. He doesn't say much in class — in fact, he's shier than most — but his artwork speaks for itself. Last month his illustration was printed on the cover of the school magazine. He's also more laid-back than you might expect for someone so talented. "I don't really stress," he says. "I just draw."

😂
Priya Sharma — Most Sociable
Year 10, age 16

If you've ever been to a school event, you've seen Priya. She's the most sociable person in the year — more confident than anyone, and funnier too. She's also surprisingly more sensitive than she looks. "People think I'm always happy," she says, "but I feel things really deeply. I'm just good at hiding it." Her friends call her the most honest person they know. "If your outfit is bad, Priya will tell you."

🌿
Finn O'Brien — Most Laid-Back
Year 10, age 15

Finn is, by a long way, the most laid-back student in the school. Nothing bothers him. He lost his phone last Tuesday and his response was "Oh well." He is, his friends admit, less reliable than Zara — he occasionally forgets to hand in homework. But he's also more generous with his time than almost anyone — always willing to help, listen, or just sit with someone who's having a bad day.

💬 Discussion — answer out loud:

1. Which person is most like you? Which is least like you?

2. The article says Priya is "more sensitive than she looks." What does that mean? Why is it interesting?

3. Finn is more generous but less reliable. Can someone be both? Do you know anyone like that?

4. If your class voted, which superlative would YOU get? (Most creative? Most laid-back? Most ambitious?) What about your best friend?
