Stage 2 · 14 min

🧠 Should have / Could have / Would have

Read Marta's story first — understand the meaning in context — then look at the form.

📖 Step 1 — Read the situation

It's September. Marta is making her second coffee of the morning, looking at the calendar on her phone. Summer is officially over.

She had all of August off — something she'd been looking forward to since January. But somehow it slipped away. Her close friend Clara moved to Lisbon two years ago and kept saying "come visit." In June Clara even sent her the link to cheap flights. Marta looked at them, thought "I'll book it next week" — and never did. "I should have just booked that flight."

There was also the ceramics course at the studio around the corner — she'd walked past the sign every week for months. She had the time. She had the money. She even told her sister she was going to sign up. She didn't. "I could have started something creative — the course ran all summer."

The first week of August she worked anyway, "just to finish a few things." By the second week she was exhausted — too tired to make plans, too restless to rest properly. She spent her evenings scrolling. "I shouldn't have let work creep into my holiday."

Now it's September. She looks at a photo Clara sent from Lisbon — sun, tiles, cold wine. And she thinks: "I would have felt so much lighter if I had actually switched off."

💬 Before we look at the grammar — what do you think Marta is feeling right now? Does any part of her summer sound familiar?

✅ Step 2 — Check the meaning (tap each question, think, then reveal)

Think about "I should have just booked that flight." Then tap to check.

Did Marta book the flight to Lisbon?
❌ No, she didn't.
Was it possible for her to book it?
✅ Yes — Clara sent her the link!
Does she think it was the right choice to not book?
❌ No — she regrets it now.
Is "should have booked" about the past or the future?
📅 The past. It's finished — she can't change it.
"I could have started the course." Did she do it?
❌ No — but it was possible. She had the time and money.
"I would have felt lighter if I had switched off." Did she switch off?
❌ No. This is an imagined, different past — it didn't happen.

📐 Step 3 — The form

All three follow the same pattern: modal + have + past participle

StructureMeaningFrom Marta's story
should have + pp It was the right thing to do — but you didn't. Regret about a missed obligation. "I should have just booked that flight."
shouldn't have + pp It was the wrong thing to do — but you did it. Regret about a mistake. "I shouldn't have let work creep into my holiday."
could have + pp It was possible — but it didn't happen. Missed opportunity. "I could have started something creative."
would have + pp This was the result in a different situation. Imagined past. "I would have felt so much lighter if I had switched off."
🔊 Pronunciation — connected speech

In natural speech have is always weak — almost swallowed. We say "should've", not two separate words. Never write "should of" — this is a spelling mistake caused by how it sounds in fast speech.

should have → "should've" /ˈʃʊdəv/ could have → "could've" /ˈkʊdəv/ would have → "would've" /ˈwʊdəv/

✏️ Controlled Practice

Produce the structures yourself — then check your answers.

Part A — Transformation (rewrite using the structure shown)

Read the situation. Rewrite it using should have, shouldn't have, could have, or would have. Think first, then reveal the model answer.

1 — should have
Marta had Clara's number. She wanted to stay in touch. She didn't call all summer. Now she misses her. → Rewrite with should have.
She should have called Clara during the summer.
Or: "She should have stayed in touch." — both are natural.
2 — shouldn't have
Iris checked her work emails every evening during her holiday. It was a mistake. She couldn't relax properly. → Rewrite with shouldn't have.
She shouldn't have checked her work emails during her holiday.
Or: "She shouldn't have taken her laptop with her."
3 — could have
There was a yoga retreat two hours from the city. It was affordable. Vera knew about it. She didn't go. → Rewrite with could have.
She could have gone to the yoga retreat — it was affordable and not far away.
Key: it was possible but she didn't take the opportunity.
4 — would have
The weather was bad in August. Because of that, the outdoor concert was cancelled. Nina was disappointed. → Rewrite with would have + if.
Nina would have loved the concert if the weather hadn't been so bad.
Pattern: would have + pp → if + hadn't / had + past participle (past perfect).
5 — your turn
Think of something from YOUR summer — a missed plan, a decision you regret, or something you wish you had done. → Make your own sentence using any of today's structures.
Example: "I should have taken a proper week off in July — I would have felt so much better going into autumn."
Now tell your teacher yours!

Part B — Error correction (find the mistake, then reveal the fix)

Each sentence has one common mistake. Can you spot it before you reveal the answer?

Error 1
"I should of booked that weekend away months ago."
✅ "I should have booked that weekend away months ago."
❌ should of is always wrong — it sounds like should've in speech, but in writing it's always should have. This is the single most common mistake with this structure.
"She could have went to Lisbon — she had the time."
Error 2
✅ "She could have gone to Lisbon."
❌ After have we always need the past participle: gone, not went. Went is simple past and can't follow have.
Error 3
"We would have enjoyed the trip more if we went earlier in the summer."
✅ "We would have enjoyed the trip more if we had gone earlier."
❌ When would have is in the main clause, the if-clause needs the past perfect: had gone — not simple past went. This is a third conditional.
Error 4
"I shouldn't have forget my passport — we nearly missed the flight!"
✅ "I shouldn't have forgotten my passport."
❌ After have we need the past participle. Forget is irregular: forget → forgot → forgotten. The base form forget can't follow have.
