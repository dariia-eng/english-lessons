🔁 Quick revision — Past simple

Regular:verb + -ed  ·  I worked, she graduated, they moved
Irregular:go → went, have → had, get → got, meet → met, leave → left
Negative:didn't + base form  ·  She didn't go travelling.
After she graduated, she got her first job in Berlin.

📐 Past perfect

Formhad + past participle
PositiveShe had left school.   They had already met.
NegativeHe hadn't gone abroad before.
QuestionHad you ever worked abroad?
UseThe earlier of two past events — it happened first, even if mentioned second.
Time wordsbefore, after, by the time, already, never, just
By the time I got there, everyone had left. ← (left = earlier)
I moved to Paris after I had finished my degree.

💡 How to combine two past events

Event 1 (earlier) She saved enough money.
Event 2 (later) She went travelling.
↓ Combine with after
After she had saved enough money, she went travelling.
[past perfect — earlier]  →  [past simple — later]
↓ Or with by the time
By the time she went travelling, she had saved enough money.
[past simple — later]  ←  [past perfect — earlier]

Other useful connectors: before, when, because + past perfect

🎯 Two Truths and a Lie

Make three sentences about your past. Two must be TRUE, one must be FALSE. Use had + past participle. Your teacher guesses which one is the lie!
Step 1 — Make your statements

Use these frames (speak your answers — don't write them!):

By the time I was [age], I had already _____ .
Before I [event], I had never _____ .
I hadn't _____ until I was [age] / until [event].
Step 2 — Teacher guesses

Which statement is the LIE?

Step 3 — Reveal!

Tell your teacher which one was the lie — and why it was false. Then swap roles: teacher makes three statements, you guess!
