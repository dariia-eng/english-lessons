Meet Sara
Read about her weekends — does any of it sound familiar?
Read the text. The words in orange are useful vocabulary — don't worry if you don't know them yet. We'll look at them together next!
Sara is 33 years old and lives in Madrid. She works in a busy office and her weeks are always full — meetings, emails, deadlines. That's why she loves her weekends so much.

On Saturday morning, she usually sleeps in until about nine o'clock. She makes a big breakfast — always coffee and toast — and reads a book or scrolls through Instagram without feeling guilty. She calls it her me time, and she protects it like it's precious.

In the afternoon, she sometimes catches up with a friend for lunch or goes for a walk in the park. She doesn't always plan things — she prefers to be a little spontaneous. If the weather is good, she might visit a market or sit in a café with her book. She tries to make the most of the daylight.

On Sunday evenings, Sara feels a little sad because the weekend is ending. She often cooks something nice for dinner and binge-watches a series to unwind. She goes to bed early so she's ready for the week. It's her quiet ritual — and she wouldn't change it for anything.

💬 Now let's talk about you:

Question 1
Sara protects her Saturday morning — she calls it her "me time". Do you have something like that in your week? What does it look like?
💡 me time · sleep in · lazy morning · ritual
Question 2
She prefers to be spontaneous on Saturday afternoons. Are you more of a planner or a last-minute person at weekends? Give an example!
💡 spontaneous · plan ahead · flexible · routine
Question 3
Sara binge-watches a series to unwind on Sundays. What do you do to switch off at the end of the weekend?
💡 unwind · recharge · binge-watch · switch off
Question 4
Do you get the "Sunday feeling" — that slight sadness when the weekend ends? What helps you deal with it?
💡 treat yourself · cook something nice · go to bed early
Question 5
If you could change one thing about your typical weekend, what would it be?
💡 make the most of · a change of scenery · catch up with · spend time

✅ Is this you? React and explain:

"I also love a lazy Saturday morning — no rush, no plans."
"I prefer spontaneous plans — I don't like scheduling my free time."
"Sunday evenings feel a little sad. I call it the 'Sunday feeling'."

📋 Teacher's note

  • ▸After each reaction, ask "And why?" or "Can you give me an example?" — don't accept one-word answers.
  • ▸The vocabulary in orange is previewed here. Students may already guess meaning from context — encourage that!
  • ▸Questions 1–5 are open — choose the ones that feel most natural for this student.

Would You Rather…?
Choose between two options — then explain why!
Click Reveal to see the question. Choose your option — then say at least two sentences to explain. There's no right answer — it's all about the conversation!
Would you rather… #1
Have a 3-day weekend every week — OR — finish work 2 hours earlier every day?
💬 Why did you choose that? What would you actually do with the extra time?
Would you rather… #2
Spend your next holiday completely alone — OR — with a big group of family and friends?
💬 Are you a solo traveller or a social one? Has this ever changed depending on how you were feeling?
Would you rather… #3
Have unlimited money but almost no free time — OR — unlimited free time but a very tight budget?
💬 What would you do with all that free time — or all that money? What does "the good life" really look like to you?
Would you rather… #4
Work from home forever — OR — always go to an office?
💬 What do you find harder — working alone, or switching off when home is also your office? What's your experience?
Would you rather… #5
Never have to do any housework again — OR — never have to cook again?
💬 Which takes more of your energy at home right now? How does it affect your weekend recharge time?

📋 Teacher's note

  • ▸Reveal the cards one at a time — let her choose which ones interest her most.
  • ▸The goal isn't to debate — it's to get her talking naturally. Push for "why" after every answer.
  • ▸These work as a jumping-off point to revisit vocabulary: "you said you'd recharge — how exactly?"

Real Talk
Agree, disagree, or it depends? Give your honest opinion!
Read each statement. Click whether you agree, disagree, or think it depends. Then explain your view — try to use at least one of today's vocabulary words!
"Everyone needs at least one day of doing absolutely nothing to feel truly happy."
💬 Follow-up: What does "doing nothing" actually look like for you? Is it really doing nothing, or is it something specific?
"It's impossible to fully disconnect from work at weekends — you always have your phone."
💬 Follow-up: Do you check work messages at weekends? Do you feel guilty if you don't — or guilty if you do?
"People who spend their whole weekend being productive are actually missing the point of life."
💬 Follow-up: Do you ever feel pressure to "use your weekend well"? Where does that pressure come from?
"As adults, we don't have enough fun. We take everything too seriously."
💬 Follow-up: What did "fun" look like when you were younger? Has it changed? What do you do now just for the joy of it?
"A weekend at home doing nothing is just as valid as a weekend full of plans and activities."
💬 Follow-up: Do you ever feel you "wasted" a weekend? What makes a weekend feel well-spent to you personally?

📋 Teacher's note

  • ▸These statements are designed to provoke genuine personal responses — there's no textbook answer.
  • ▸Push for examples: "Can you think of a time when that was true for you?"
  • ▸If she agrees with everything, challenge gently: "Really? Can you think of anyone who might disagree?"
  • ▸Useful phrase to model: "I agree to some extent, but…" / "It depends on the situation — for example…"
