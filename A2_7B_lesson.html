🧑‍🤝‍🧑 Who are freegans?

Some people look for food that supermarkets and cafés throw away — instead of buying it. These people are called freegans. They believe that our society wastes too much food, so they try to find and use food that others throw out.

Before reading: What do you think about freegans? Is this a good idea? Would you do the same?

📰 Don't waste it – eat for free!

It's early evening in downtown New York and it's closing time for many cafés and supermarkets. It's time to throw out all their old food for the day. A group of young people arrive and quickly start to look for any food they can find.

These people are freegans. They're people who think that we throw out a lot of food, so they try to use it.

'It's amazing what supermarkets throw away,' says Elias, a 24-year-old. 'Yesterday I found a box of eggs, with just one broken egg, and four frozen pizzas. They were fine, but the boxes were broken so the supermarket threw them away.'

So how much food do they find? And how many meals can they make? Elias says sometimes they find a little, and sometimes they find a lot, but they're never hungry and can always make a meal.

📚 Grammar: How much? / How many? + Quantifiers

How MUCH? → Uncountable nouns

Use with nouns you can't count:

water, oil, milk, rice, bread, coffee, meat, sugar, fruit

→ How much oil do we have?

→ How much milk did you buy?

How MANY? → Countable nouns

Use with nouns you can count:

eggs, pizzas, apples, meals, bottles, bags

→ How many eggs did you find?

→ How many apples do you want?

some eggs  /  some oil

lots of eggs  /  a lot of oil
💡 Remember: Use a few (countable) → a few bananas  |  Use a little (uncountable) → a little cheese

4. "There's a little coffee." Is there a lot of coffee?
