Stage 2 · Language Focus
Cleft Sentences

Two Cleft Types

1 — It-Cleft

It + be + [emphasised information] + who / that + [rest of sentence]
"It's the unemployed who are worst off."
"It was at the last meeting that we discussed this."
"It's on Clarence Road that we'd like speed bumps."

→ Highlights a specific person, time, place, or thing — contrasting it with what the listener might expect.

2 — What-Cleft (Pseudo-Cleft)

What + [subject + verb] + is/was + [new / emphasised information]
"What I'm saying is that it's important to plan ahead."
"What we did was set up a committee."
"What I hate is being ignored."
"All we want is cheaper homes."

→ The what-clause contains known or background information; the part after is/was is the new, highlighted point.

Side by Side

IT-CLEFT
"It was the council that approved the plan."
Highlights: who approved it
WHAT-CLEFT
"What the council did was approve the plan."
Highlights: what they did

Stage 3 · Pronunciation
Weak Form of that

Weak Form: that /ðət/

In spoken English, the conjunction that is almost always unstressed — it's pronounced with a weak schwa /ə/ sound: /ðət/, not /ðæt/. This is especially common in cleft sentences after is/was.

Strong (for emphasis/contrast): "I know that" → /ðæt/
Weak (in normal speech): "What I'm saying is that /ðət/ we need to act."
Exercise 8a — Read aloud, then reveal
Read each sentence aloud before clicking. Try to use the weak /ðət/ naturally. Your teacher will give feedback on your linking and stress. Click to see how connected speech works in practice.
1. "What I'm saying is that we have to invest in the future."
Fast speech: "What I'm saying's-that we…" — is contracts and that is barely audible.
2. "It's the residents that will benefit most."
Fast speech: "It's the residents-that will…" — that links smoothly into the next word.
3. "I believe that this is the right decision."
Very reduced: "I believe-that this…" — so reduced it may sound like a final /t/ on "believe".
4. "What we want is that this project moves forward."
After is: "What we want's-that…" — both words reduced together.
5. "We all know that cuts have consequences."
Smooth linking: "We all know-that cuts…"
🔊 Exercise 8b — Listen and repeat drill
  1. Your teacher reads each sentence — repeat it immediately, matching the rhythm and weak /ðət/.
  2. Then swap: you read, your teacher repeats and gives feedback on any over-stressed that.
  3. Finally, say this sentence from memory three times, getting faster each time: "What I'm saying is that it's the community that matters most."
