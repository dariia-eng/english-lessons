Countable and uncountable nouns

Countable nouns — you can count them (1, 2, 3…)
✅ They have a singular and a plural form.
an egg → two eggs  ·  a bean → some beans  ·  a banana → three bananas
Uncountable nouns — you cannot count them.
❌ They have no plural form. Use a singular verb.
rice → some rice (NOT two rices)  ·  milk → some milk (NOT a milk)
⚠️ Watch out! Some words can be countable or uncountable depending on the context. Coffee is usually uncountable, but "two coffees please" (= two cups) is also possible in a café.

some  ·  any  ·  lots of / a lot of

Use some in positive sentences (countable + uncountable).
There's some bread.  ·  I've got some eggs.
Use any in negative sentences and questions (countable + uncountable).
There aren't any vegetables.  ·  Is there any coffee?
Use lots of / a lot of for a large amount (countable + uncountable).
There's lots of pasta.  ·  I buy a lot of soft drinks.
