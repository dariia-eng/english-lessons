Stage 3

Grammar — Guided Discovery

🔍 Discover the rules — look at Rafaela's sentences

Read each group of sentences from what Rafaela said. Answer the questions to discover the grammar rule, then check.

Group 1 — adverb position
a. I'll definitely try to make a deal with the theatre.
b. I'm probably going to spend most of the money on Gilda.
c. I don't think I'll pay them a fee. ✅   vs.   I think I won't pay them a fee. ✗
1. In sentence (a), where does definitely go — before or after will?
✅ Before will — I'll definitely try…  |  She'll probably come. The adverb goes between will and the main verb.
2. What about with won't? Look at (c): which is more natural — don't think I'll or think I won't?
✅ I don't think I'll… is much more natural in English. We prefer to put the negative on think, not on will.
Group 2 — expressing probability
a. It's unlikely that I'll find someone at such short notice.
b. It's quite likely that she'll say yes.
c. I doubt I'll set up a website.
d. I may well pay my brother to help.   |   I might ask him to help instead.
3. Complete this rule: It's unlikely that… has the same meaning as I ___ think I'll…
✅ don't — "It's unlikely that I'll find someone" = "I don't think I'll find someone."
4. Put these in order from most certain → least certain: definitely · probably · may well · might · doubt
✅ definitely → probably → may well → might → I doubt / It's unlikely
Group 3 — future plans in progress
a. I'm planning to sell tickets for £15 each.
b. I'm thinking about doing a pop-up café.
c. I'm wondering whether to call the whole thing off.
5. All three sentences are in the present continuous. What does this tell us about these plans?
✅ The plans are still being considered or decided — they're in progress, not final. Using be + -ing emphasises that the thinking is ongoing right now.
6. Which structure needs to + infinitive, which needs a gerund (-ing), and which needs whether to + infinitive?
✅ planning to + infinitive  |  thinking about + -ing  |  wondering whether to + infinitive

Stage 5

Pronunciation — Word Stress

6a Word stress — which syllable is stressed?

Look at these words from the lesson. Underline the stressed syllable — then tap each word to check.

💡 Stress patterns to notice:
• Words ending in -tion / -sion — stress on the syllable before: or·gan·i·ZA·tion, pro·MO·tion
• Words ending in -ity / -ify — stress two syllables before: po·SIB·i·li·ty, pro·BAB·ly
• In compound nouns like business plan — stress usually falls on the first word: BUS·i·ness plan
6b Practise saying them

With a partner, take turns. One person says a word, the other marks the stress. Then check together using the cards above.

Try these sentences. Tap to mark where you think the main stress falls:

  • She's going to organise a concert next weekend.
  • It's unlikely that she'll make a profit.
  • I'm planning to promote the event on social media.
  • I doubt we'll find someone to take her place.
