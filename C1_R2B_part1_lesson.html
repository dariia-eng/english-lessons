📄 Reading 8 minutes

The Job Title Revolution

Read the text quickly (2 min), then answer the T/F/N questions below. Discuss your answers with a partner.

Over the past decade, something unusual has been happening in offices around the world. The humble 'cleaner' has become a 'Sanitation Engineer'. The 'receptionist' is now a 'Director of First Impressions'. And if you work in sales, there's a reasonable chance your business card reads 'Customer Success Evangelist'.

Companies argue that elevated job titles improve morale and attract better candidates. Employees, they claim, are likely to feel more invested in their work when their role sounds impressive. Cynics, however, suggest a different motivation: it's far cheaper to give someone a grander title than a pay rise.

There is some evidence that titles matter. Studies suggest people are certain to describe their work differently depending on how it's labelled — a hospital cleaner who sees their role as 'keeping patients safe from infection' reports higher satisfaction than one who simply 'mops floors'. Whether this justifies renaming everyone or not, one thing is highly unlikely: this trend is not going away anytime soon.

True (T), False (F), or Not Given (N)?
Mini-discussion

Do you think giving employees more impressive titles is a genuine benefit — or just a trick? Have you ever experienced this yourself?

Pattern 1 — emphasise probability
Start with "It"
It's highly likely that our jobs will change.
It was unlikely that she knew about it.
It's certain that salaries will rise.
Pattern 2 — emphasise subject
Subject + probability + infinitive
Our jobs are highly likely to change.
She was unlikely to have known about it.
Salaries are certain to rise.

🗣️ Speaking 8 minutes

Say Something True

Complete each sentence starter so that it's true for you. Write a note if it helps. Then share with a partner — ask one follow-up question after each one.

"I'm highly unlikely to … in my career."
Think about a job, a task, or a decision.
"It's certain that within ten years, my job / field will …"
Use what you know about your industry or area of study.
"Most people in my workplace or class are likely to … by the end of this year."
Think about plans, challenges, or changes.
"It's highly unlikely that … — and honestly, I think that's a good / bad thing."
This could be about work, technology, or society.
Follow-up questions

"Why is that?" · "Has that always been the case?" · "Do you think that's a good thing?" · "What would you change if you could?"
