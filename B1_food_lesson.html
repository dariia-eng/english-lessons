Meet Clara
Read about her relationship with food — does any of it sound familiar?
Read the text. The words in orange are today's vocabulary — you'll practise them properly in the next section. For now, just read and enjoy!
Clara is 34 years old and lives in Valencia. She works in marketing and her weeks are fast, stressful, and rarely predictable. Her relationship with food? Complicated — but she wouldn't have it any other way.

During the week, Clara barely has time to think about cooking. She usually grabs a bite at the office — a sandwich, a salad from the place around the corner, or leftovers from whatever she whipped up the night before. She doesn't skip meals — she's learned that the hard way — but she doesn't always eat out either. "Too much time," she says. "And too much money."

But on weekends, everything changes. Saturday is when Clara becomes the person she actually wants to be in the kitchen. She puts music on, pours a glass of wine, and cooks a proper home-cooked meal — something that takes time, something that fills the flat with smell. It's her version of therapy.

She definitely has a sweet tooth. There's always chocolate somewhere in her flat — usually dark, always hidden badly. Her biggest guilty pleasure? Ordering churros and eating them straight from the bag on the sofa while watching something terrible on Netflix. She calls it comfort food, and she has absolutely no regrets.

💬 Now let's talk about you:

Clara barely cooks during the week. What's your typical weekday relationship with food — do you cook, grab something quick, or skip meals entirely?
💡 grab a bite · skip a meal · whip up · home-cooked
Question 2

💡 unwind · recharge · enjoy · hate / love cooking
Question 3
Do you have a sweet tooth, or are you more of a savoury person? What's your go-to snack?
💡 sweet tooth · guilty pleasure · comfort food
Question 4
What's your biggest food guilty pleasure? Something you eat even though maybe you "shouldn't"?
💡 guilty pleasure · comfort food · no regrets
Question 5
If you could only cook one meal for someone to impress them, what would it be?
💡 home-cooked meal · whip up · recipe · speciality

✅ Is this you? React and explain:

"I'm too busy during the week to cook properly — I just eat whatever's easiest."
"Cooking at the weekend feels completely different — it's actually enjoyable."
"I have hidden snacks somewhere in my house right now."

📋 Teacher's note

  • ▸The third reaction is deliberately light — it usually gets a laugh and relaxes any tension.
  • ▸Q2 and Q4 tend to generate the most personal responses — give her time with those.
  • ▸If she talks about Spanish food specifically, follow that thread — it's gold.

Would You Rather…?
Food dilemmas — choose and explain!
Reveal the question, pick your option, and say at least two sentences to explain your choice.
Would you rather… #1
Only ever eat at home for the rest of your life — OR — only ever eat out?
💬 What would you miss most about the option you didn't choose?
Would you rather… #2
Give up coffee forever — OR — give up alcohol forever?
💬 Be honest — how much would that actually affect your daily life or social life?
Would you rather… #3
Know how to cook one dish absolutely perfectly — OR — be able to make anything edible?
💬 If you chose one perfect dish — what would it be? And why that one?
Would you rather… #4
Eat your favourite meal every single day — OR — never eat it again but try something new every day?
💬 What's the favourite meal you'd be sacrificing — or what new cuisine would you explore first?

📋 Teacher's note

  • ▸These are deliberately fun — the goal is fluency, not accuracy. Let her speak freely.
  • ▸Card 2 (coffee vs. alcohol) usually gets a strong reaction from Spanish speakers — great conversation starter.
  • ▸After each answer, push for more: "Would you really though? Tell me more about that."

Real Talk
Agree, disagree, or it depends? Say what you really think!
Read each statement and react honestly. Click your answer — then explain your view in a few sentences. Try to use at least one vocabulary word!
"Eating alone is actually quite nice — it's peaceful and you can fully enjoy the food."
💬 Follow-up: Do you eat alone often? What do you usually do at the same time — phone, TV, book, nothing?
"Cooking is a form of self-care — it's just as relaxing as a walk or a bath."
💬 Follow-up: What would make cooking feel more like a pleasure and less like a chore for you?
"Food delivery apps have ruined people's ability to cook. Nobody learns anymore."
💬 Follow-up: Do you use food delivery apps? Be honest — how often, and do you feel any guilt about it?
"People who eat at their desk during lunch break are working too hard — or not setting good boundaries."
💬 Follow-up: What does your lunch break actually look like? Is it a real break, or do you just eat quickly and carry on?
"There is no such thing as a guilty pleasure when it comes to food. You should enjoy what you enjoy."
💬 Follow-up: What's your actual "guilty" food pleasure — the thing you eat when nobody's watching?

📋 Teacher's note

  • ▸Statement 4 (desk lunch) is very relatable for a busy working adult — it often unlocks a real, personal conversation.
  • ▸Statement 5 usually gets a strong opinion — either "absolutely" or "but health though…" Either way, great for discussion.
  • ▸Useful phrase to model if she's hesitant: "Well, I sort of agree, but…" / "That's interesting — in Spain it's…"
