Stage 1 · Warm-up5 min

Two countries, one language?

Let's chat about English around the world.

  • Which series, films or YouTubers do you watch in English? Are they British or American?
  • Can you hear the difference between a British and an American accent? Try to copy both!
  • Which accent do you like more? Why?
  • Do you know any words that are different in the UK and the USA?
  • In Spain and Latin America people speak Spanish. Do they use some different words too? Give an example!
Teacher notes
  • Keep it short: the accent work happens in the video stage next.
  • The Spanish question (e.g. ordenador/computadora, coche/carro, zumo/jugo) helps her see that varieties are normal and neither one is "wrong".

Stage 9 · Speaking10 minSpin & speak

Spin & speak

Spin to get a question and a mode. Answer in at least 4 sentences. If the mode is UK, use only British words. If it's US, use only American words. If you use a word from the wrong country, your teacher gets a point!

Press Spin to get your first question.
Teacher notes
  • Answer each question yourself too, so it feels like a real conversation.
  • Ask at least one follow-up per question (Why? When? Who with? Have you ever…?). This also recycles tenses from the last lesson.
Stage 10 · Role-play15 min

Tourist helper

Two scenes. In scene A you help an American tourist in London. In scene B you're a British tourist in New York.

You are a London guide. Your teacher is a confused American tourist. Answer each question, and teach the tourist the British word: "Ah, in Britain we call it…"

    You are a British tourist in New York. Use the British words on your card. Your teacher is a New Yorker who only knows American words. When they don't understand, explain: "It's a thing you…"

      Useful phrases

      Excuse me, could you tell me where…?Do you mean…?In Britain we say…, but here you say…Sorry, what's a…?It's just around the corner.Go straight on / turn left.
      Teacher notes
      • A: read the tourist lines yourself and act very confused. Reveal the help only if she gets stuck.
      • B: she reads her line, and you reply in American English. She must notice the new word and repeat it: "Oh, so here it's 'fries'!"
      • If time allows, replay one scene with roles reversed.
