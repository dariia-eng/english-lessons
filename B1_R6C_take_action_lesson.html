Section 2 · Reading

How Can You Help?

🧴
The Plastic & Fashion Crisis

Every minute, the equivalent of a rubbish truck full of plastic enters the ocean. Microplastics — tiny particles from bottles and synthetic clothing — have now been found in human blood, rainwater, and even the deepest parts of the ocean.

Fast fashion makes it worse: 85% of all textiles end up in landfill each year. Buying just one second-hand item instead of new saves 3 kg of CO₂.

8M tonnes of plastic enter oceans yearly
🐘
Wildlife Under Threat

Scientists estimate we are losing up to 150 species every single day — a rate 1,000 times faster than the natural background extinction rate. Habitat destruction, hunting, and climate change are the main causes.

However, conservation works: tiger populations have actually grown by 40% since 2010, and the bald eagle was taken off the endangered list thanks to decades of protection.

1M species face extinction this century
🌫️
Air Pollution & Digital Carbon

Air pollution kills approximately 7 million people each year — more than malaria, TB, and AIDS combined. Most of this comes from burning fossil fuels for energy and transport.

Surprisingly, the internet also has a carbon footprint: data centres currently use about 1–2% of global electricity. Streaming one hour of HD video emits around 36 grams of CO₂ — and we watch over 1 billion hours of video online every day.

Data centres: as much CO₂ as aviation

Section 4 · Carbon Footprint

What's Your CO₂ Footprint?

🌍 Did you know? The average global CO₂ footprint is 4.7 tonnes per person per year. To limit global warming to 1.5°C, scientists say we need to get to under 2 tonnes by 2050. The EU average is ~8t, the US is ~14t, and India is ~1.9t.

Section 5 · Grammar

Question Tags

📐 The Rule

+→−Positive statement → negative tag: "Animals are beautiful, aren't they?"
−→+Negative statement → positive tag: "We don't recycle enough, do we?"
KEYThe tag uses the same auxiliary verb as the main clause (be, do, have, can, should, will…)
⚡ Think of it like a battery
＋
Positive statement
"Animals are beautiful…"
→ TAG ←
−
Negative tag
"…aren't they?"
−
Negative statement
"We don't recycle enough…"
→ TAG ←
＋
Positive tag
"…do we?"
Just like a battery — the two ends are always opposite. A positive statement needs a negative tag, and a negative statement needs a positive tag. The tag always uses the same auxiliary verb as the statement.

🔍 Spot the Mistake — correct the wrong question tag

1. "Plastic is one of the biggest pollutants, do they?"

❌ do they → ✅ isn't it  (positive statement → negative tag; subject is "plastic" → "it")

2. "She doesn't care about recycling, doesn't she?"

❌ doesn't she → ✅ does she  (negative statement → positive tag)

3. "We can make a difference, can we?"

❌ can we → ✅ can't we  (positive statement → negative tag)

4. "There aren't enough nature reserves, aren't there?"

❌ aren't there → ✅ are there  (negative statement → positive tag)

Section 7 · Beyond the Environment

The Other Global Crises

Climate change is urgent — but it's not the only crisis our world faces. Wars, hunger, and lack of education affect hundreds of millions of people today. These problems are deeply connected: conflict destroys food systems; poverty prevents children from going to school; inequality fuels unrest. Understanding them is the first step to acting.
⚔️

Armed Conflict & Peace

Global Peace Index 2024

According to the Institute for Economics and Peace, only 9 countries in the world are currently free from any conflict. Armed conflict costs the global economy an estimated $17.5 trillion per year — about 12% of global GDP.

  • The most peaceful country: 🇮🇸 Iceland (since 2008)
  • The least peaceful: 🇾🇪 Yemen, 🇸🇸 South Sudan, 🇸🇾 Syria
  • Over 100 million people are currently displaced
  • Conflicts today are more about resources, religion & inequality
🍽️

Hunger & Food Security

733M people face hunger daily

The world produces enough food for 10 billion people, yet 733 million go hungry. The problem isn't production — it's distribution, waste, and inequality.

  • 1/3 of all food produced is wasted every year
  • Climate change is reducing crop yields in vulnerable regions
  • 2.3 billion people face moderate food insecurity
  • Sub-Saharan Africa most affected; Ukraine war hit global grain supply
📚

Education Access

244M children out of school

Education is the most powerful tool for breaking the cycle of poverty — yet 244 million children worldwide don't attend school, and 750 million adults cannot read.

  • Girls are disproportionately affected, especially in conflict zones
  • For every $1 invested in education, societies gain up to $10 back
  • Climate change is closing schools: heatwaves and floods disrupt learning
  • Digital divide: millions lack internet for online education

💡 What Can We Do as Individuals?

🤝
Support NGOs

Donate to or volunteer with organisations like Oxfam, UNICEF, Save the Children, Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF), or the Red Cross.

🗳️
Vote & Advocate

Choose politicians who prioritise foreign aid, refugee rights, and conflict prevention. Write to your local representatives.

🛍️
Ethical Consumption

Avoid products linked to conflict minerals (e.g. some electronics). Choose Fairtrade food and clothing made in safe conditions.

🍞
Reduce Food Waste

Plan meals, use leftovers, and compost. If every family halved their food waste, we could feed millions more people.

📖
Sponsor a Child's Education

Organisations like Plan International or ActionAid let you sponsor a child's schooling for as little as €25/month.

🌐
Raise Awareness

Share accurate information on social media. Use your platform — however small — to keep these issues visible and part of the conversation.

📖 Key Vocabulary Glossary

reduce
make something smaller in size or amount
protect
keep safe from harm or damage
destroy
damage so badly it no longer exists
support
help or give resources to something
damage
cause harm or injury to something
recycle
process used items into new products
reuse
use something again instead of throwing it away
waste
use more than necessary; also: discarded material
conservation
protecting nature and wildlife from harm
microplastics
tiny plastic particles that pollute environments
displaced
forced to leave your home, usually by conflict
carbon footprint
total CO₂ produced by a person's actions
