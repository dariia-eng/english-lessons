Stage 3 · Reading10 min

3 viral food trends

Read the three short texts. Then answer the questions.

🧋

Bubble tea

Bubble tea comes from Taiwan, where people started making it in the 1980s. It's tea with milk or fruit, ice, and little balls called tapioca pearls. The pearls are soft and chewy, and you drink them through a very big straw. Some people love it, but others say it's too sweet and the pearls are too weird.

🍫

Dubai chocolate

Dubai chocolate is a thick chocolate bar filled with pistachio cream and crispy shredded pastry. It became famous on TikTok, and in 2024 it was so popular in Germany that many shops sold out. Fans say it's crunchy, creamy and addictive. Critics say it's too expensive for a chocolate bar.

🍬

Giant sweets

Giant gummy bears, huge lollipops and jelly sweets as big as your head: giant sweets are perfect for videos because they look crazy. But are they really good? One giant gummy bear can have as much sugar as a whole bag of normal ones. It's fun for a video, but it's probably too big for one person!

Did you understand?

Teacher notes
  • Ask her to find every too + adjective in the texts (too sweet, too weird, too expensive, too big). These set up Stage 5.

Stage 5 · Grammar20 min

Too sweet? Sweet enough?

"This bubble tea is too sweet." = more sugar than I want 👎 · "This chocolate isn't crunchy enough." = less than I want 👎 · "It's sweet enough." = just right 👍

🥶not hot enoughless than you want
😋hot enoughjust right
🥵too hotmore than you want

Rule card · complete it

too + adjectiveIt's sweet. (= more than I want)
adjective + enoughIt isn't crunchy (enough comes after the adjective!)
enough + nounThere isn't sugar. (enough comes before a noun)
too much + uncountableThere's too cream.
too many + countableThere are too pearls.
+ to + verbI'm too full eat dessert. · She's old enough to drive.

A · Choose the correct option

B · Say it another way

Rewrite the sentence with the word in brackets. Keep the same meaning.

2The chocolate isn't cheap enough. (expensive)

Teacher notes
  • German trap: zu = too, but genug goes after adjectives in English too ("gut genug" = good enough). Easy! But ✗ enough good still appears. Check it.
  • Don't mix up too (zu) / to (zu, nach) / two (zwei). They sound the same!
