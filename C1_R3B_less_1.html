3Reading

Reading

6 problem-solving techniques that work

We all face problems — at work, at home, in relationships. But some people seem to handle them better than others. What's their secret? Here are six techniques that really work.

1. Take stock of the situation. Before you do anything, pause. Understand exactly what you're dealing with. Many people rush into solutions before they've fully grasped the problem.

2. Pin it down. Vague problems are hard to solve. Get specific. What exactly is wrong? When does it happen? Who is involved? Once you pin it down, the solution often becomes clearer.

3. Find the root cause. The presenting problem is rarely the real one. Dig deeper. Ask "why?" five times and you'll usually find the root cause hiding underneath.

4. Allow time for mulling over. Not everything needs an immediate answer. Sometimes the best thing is to sleep on it. Your brain keeps working on the problem while you're doing something else — this is mulling over.

5. Avoid misinterpreting the data. We see what we expect to see. Be careful not to misinterpret the information in front of you. Ask someone else to look at it with fresh eyes.

6. Trust your intuition. After you've done the analysis, sometimes you need to trust your gut. If something feels wrong, it probably is. Don't ignore that feeling — but do check it against the facts.

The next time you feel befuddled by a problem or find yourself going round in circles, try one of these techniques. You might be surprised how quickly things become clearer.

Teacher notes
Allow S to read independently (3–4 min). Then ask: "Which technique do you already use? Which would you like to try?" Don't pre-teach vocabulary yet — that comes in Stage 4.

Part B — Case Situations (10 min):

Read each situation. Which problem-solving technique from the reading would help most? Why? Is there more than one good answer?

Case 1

Your team has been in meetings about the same project for three weeks. Every time you reach a decision, someone raises a new concern and you start over. Nothing is moving forward.

Teacher's suggested technique ▼

🎯 Pin it down + Take stock — the team is going round in circles because no one has defined exactly what decision needs to be made. Pinning down the specific question and taking stock of what's actually been agreed would break the deadlock.

Case 2

A colleague keeps missing deadlines. You've spoken to them twice and they promise to improve, but nothing changes. You're frustrated and don't know what to do next.

Teacher's suggested technique ▼

🎯 Root cause — the repeated missing of deadlines is a symptom. Is the colleague overloaded? Do they misunderstand priorities? Until you find the root cause, any solution will be temporary.

Case 3

You've been offered a great job — better pay, interesting work — but it means moving to another city. You've been going back and forth for two weeks and still can't decide. You feel completely befuddled.

Teacher's suggested technique ▼

🎯 Mull over + Trust your intuition — sometimes overanalysing creates paralysis. After sleeping on it properly, your gut feeling becomes clearer. You could also take stock by listing what actually matters to you.
