Why Can't We Stop Buying?

We live in a world designed to make us buy more. Adverts, social media, and next-day delivery have made it easier than ever to get new things — and to want them. The result? Homes full of clutter, and people who feel overwhelmed by everything they own. We live in a throwaway culture: we buy cheaply, use things briefly, then throw them away. The average home today contains around 300,000 items, from paperclips to furniture. We own more possessions than at any point in human history, and yet most people still feel like they don't have enough. So why is it so hard to stop?

Psychologists say that shopping gives us a short burst of happiness. When we buy something new, our brain releases a chemical called dopamine — the same chemical that makes us feel good when we eat something delicious or hear our favourite song. The problem is that this feeling doesn't last long. Soon, we want something else. Some researchers even call this dopamine shopping — buying not for the product itself, but for the feeling of buying it. That jacket you didn't plan to buy? That is an impulse buy, and dopamine is why.

The minimalist movement is a response to this cycle. Minimalism means living with only what you really need. Many people choose to declutter their homes — removing everything they don't use or love. Some prefer to buy second-hand rather than new, finding that quality items last longer and cost less. Without so much extra stuff, minimalists argue, you have less to worry about, more time for the things that really matter, and more money to spend on experiences rather than objects.

But is minimalism for everyone? Critics point out that for many people, buying new things isn't just about dopamine — it can reflect culture, identity, or simply the joy of creativity and self-expression. The key, most experts agree, is to become a conscious consumer: someone who thinks before they buy, considers the environmental impact, and chooses quality over quantity. Making sustainable choices — buying less, buying better — is something everyone can try, whatever their lifestyle.
