Stage 3 · Reading
A Letter to the Editor
Read the full letter. Pay attention to the highlighted words — they carry strong negative connotations. Think about how the writer could express the same ideas more neutrally.
Dear Editor,

I am writing to express my deep frustration with the state of public transport in our city. The problems are now so severe that commuters are left with no real choice but to use their cars, causing even greater congestion.

The bus network is archaic — many routes were designed decades ago and have never been updated to reflect how the city has grown. Timetables are chaotic, with buses arriving unpredictably and sometimes not at all. Standing room only seems to be the norm, not the exception.

Most infuriating of all is the attitude of the local council. Their penny-pinching approach to public services means that investment in new buses and better infrastructure has been postponed year after year. Meanwhile, they ask residents to make difficult sacrifices — higher fares, fewer routes, longer waits.

I urge the council to stop treating public transport as an inconvenient expense and start seeing it as a civic responsibility.

— A frustrated resident

Comprehension — True, False or Not Given?
1. The writer argues that poor transport is making the city's traffic worse.
2. The council has recently invested in new buses.
3. The letter writer takes the bus to work every day.
4. The writer wants the council to treat public transport as a duty to residents.

💬 Discuss

  • How would the tone change if the writer used vintage, relaxed, economical, challenging, issues?
  • Who is more persuasive — someone who uses emotive language, or someone who stays neutral?

Stage 6 · Language Focus
Cleft Sentences — First Look
Audio 2.9 — Listen to extracts from the interview. Complete the cleft sentence rewrites. Each rewrites the original sentence to emphasise one piece of information more strongly.
Exercise 7a — Complete the cleft sentences
Original: We looked at ways to improve the transport network.
 we   to look at were ways to improve the transport network.
💡 Start with the question word that introduces known info. What verb fills the second gap?
Original: I'm saying that we have to invest in infrastructure.
 I'm saying   we have to invest in infrastructure.
💡 First gap: the question word. Second gap: verb + conjunction.
Original: My colleague made that statement.
 was my colleague   made that statement.
💡 First gap: a pronoun. Second gap: a relative pronoun (two options).
Original: I can't stand having to make cuts.
 I can't stand   having to make cuts.
💡 First gap: the question word. Second gap: a linking verb.

🔍 Notice

Sentences 1, 2 and 4 begin with What — the known information comes first, and the new, emphasised point follows after is/was/were. Sentence 3 begins with It — this highlights a specific person or thing. Both patterns will be practised in full in Lesson 2.
