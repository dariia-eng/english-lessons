My co-workers sometimes ask me if I'm married, how much I earn, who I vote for, etc. Those questions are fine in their culture but I'm not comfortable answering them. Should I answer them anyway?

Stage 5

Grammar — Introductory It

Discovery What does It refer to?

Look at these sentences from the lesson. It is highlighted. Answer the questions, then tap Reveal rule to check.

Pattern 1 — It + verb + that
a. It appears that she's new to this.
b. It seems that you're unhappy.
What does It refer to in these sentences? Is it a thing or a person?
Rule: It refers forward to the that-clause. It is a dummy subject — it doesn't refer to a thing or a person. Verbs like appear, seem, turn out, follow use this pattern.
Pattern 2 — It + verb + person + that
a. It occurs to me that this is a culture clash.
b. It strikes me that she's in a very difficult situation.
Who does me refer to? Why is It used instead of naming the clause directly?
Rule: It + verb + person + that describes what a situation makes someone think or feel. Verbs: strike, feel, occur to, seem, bother, concern, surprise, alarm, amaze.
Pattern 3 — It + verb + like / as if / as though
a. It sounds like he wants to avoid an argument.
b. It looks like she wants to make friends with her colleagues.
These sentences describe how situations appear. Which word gives us that meaning of appearance/impression?
Rule: It + verb + like / as if / as though describes how a situation seems or appears to you. Verbs: feel, look, seem, sound.
Pattern 4 — It + verb + person + infinitive with to
a. It surprises me to see that she's lost.
b. It bothers me to hear they haven't sorted this out yet.
What comes after the person in these sentences — a that-clause or an infinitive?
Rule: It + verb + person + infinitive with to (+that) describes what something makes you think or how it makes you feel. Verbs: bother, surprise, alarm, amaze.

📌 Introductory It — Summary

1 It + verb + that → It appears that she's new to this.
2 It + verb + person + that → It occurs to me that this is a culture clash.
3 It + verb + like / as if / as though → It sounds like he wants to avoid an argument.
4 It + verb + person + infinitive with to → It surprises me to see that she's lost.
Note — it's not that / it's just that:
It's not that I like it; it's just that I don't mind it.
Use it's not that… to deny something and it's just that… to explain the real reason.
